พระเครื่อง

Phra Khrueang

Buddhist amulets

The general term for Buddhist amulets: small sacred images, commonly of the Buddha or a revered monk, carried or worn.

Everyday usage: In an amulet conversation, people often shorten this to พระ (Phra). The full term makes the meaning explicit; outside this context, Phra can refer to a monk or a sacred image.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗
Explore meaning & tradition

The main Buddhist-amulet term. Phra khrueang is the key general term for Buddhist amulets, commonly small sacred images carried or worn. It is broader than the name of a single model such as Phra Somdet or Phra Pit Ta, but more specific than the English word “amulet” used for charms of many kinds.

Forms and materials. Thai historical writing connects worn Buddhist amulets with moulded votive tablets deposited in stupas. Clay, powder mixtures, and cast metal are important distinctions between forms. An image’s design and its material can therefore be named separately.

Useful neighbouring words. Phra phim emphasizes a moulded image or tablet; rup lo emphasizes a cast image; rian identifies a medal. Phra bucha usually identifies an image intended for an altar. These terms help you recognize what kind of sacred object is being discussed.

Related: Phra Phim · Phra Bucha · Watthu Mongkhon

Umbrella term · Foundation

เครื่องราง

Khrueang Rang

Charms

A charm carried, worn, or kept for protection or good fortune.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗
Explore meaning & tradition

Khrueang rang covers many forms, including takrut scrolls, cowrie-shell charms, fangs and carved figures. A charm can be carried, worn or kept in a home, shop or shrine.

Its name may describe its form, material or intended purpose. Takrut identifies a rolled inscribed charm; metta maha niyom identifies goodwill and popularity.

In collecting, khrueang rang is often distinguished from phra khrueang, Buddhist amulets. In broader speech, the protective meaning of khrueang rang can include Buddhist amulets too.

Related: Khong Khlang · Watthu Mongkhon · Takrut

ของขลัง

Khong Khlang

Sacred objects

Objects regarded as possessing sacred or protective power.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗
Explore meaning & tradition

Khong khlang describes sacred potency rather than a particular shape or material. Buddhist amulets, consecrated charms, unusual natural objects and sacred plants all belong to this wider vocabulary.

Phra khrueang names Buddhist amulets; khrueang rang names charms; watthu mongkhon emphasizes auspiciousness. The expressions overlap, and a single object can be described with more than one of them.

Umbrella term · Foundation

วัตถุมงคล

Watthu Mongkhon

Auspicious sacred objects

A broad term for objects regarded as sacred or auspicious, including amulets and charms.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗

Related: Khong Khlang · Khrueang Rang · Phra Khrueang

Explore meaning & tradition

An umbrella expression. Watthu means an object or thing; mongkhon expresses auspiciousness. Together, watthu mongkhon is a broad expression for objects regarded as auspicious or sacred. It is useful when talking about a range of items without naming one particular object type.

How it relates to other names. A Buddhist amulet can be called both phra khrueang and watthu mongkhon. An inscribed charm may be named by its form, such as takrut, and also described as an auspicious object. The broad expression and the specific object name answer different questions.

What the label tells you. This name concerns the object’s cultural or devotional status. Its shape, ingredients, edition, maker, and associated beliefs still need their own explanation. It provides an entry point into the vocabulary rather than a complete description of an individual item.

ยันต์

Yant

Sacred diagram

A sacred diagram combining lines, letters, numbers, or images.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗
Explore meaning & tradition

Diagram, writing, and symbol. Yant is a sacred diagram combining lines, characters, numbers, or images. It can appear on cloth, metal, manuscripts, or the body. This is a foundational term: the surface and purpose are additional parts of an individual description.

Reading the relationships. Pha yant names a diagram on cloth, while sak yant names tattooing sacred diagrams or inscriptions. Khatha refers to recited words; akkhara refers to characters. These are connected parts of ritual knowledge, rather than four names for the same thing.

Interpreting a design. Characters can represent syllables or shortened formulas. Script and language also need separate identification: Khom lettering may write Pali. Reading a design therefore involves its form, its writing, and the tradition that explains it.

Related: Sak Yant · Akkhara · Akson Khom · Akson Tham Lanna

คาถา

Khatha

Incantation

A verse or incantation recited in religious or magical practice.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗
Explore meaning & tradition

Words used in recitation. Khatha names a verse or incantation used in religious or magical practice. Its spoken or recited form distinguishes it from the diagram itself. Recitation can accompany making, consecrating, or using a sacred object, and can also accompany sacred tattooing.

Language and writing. Pali is important in many recitations. A text’s language and its script are different: Pali can be written in Thai, Khom, or other scripts. The appearance of unfamiliar letters does not, by itself, tell an English reader which language is being used.

Short formulas. Hua chai khatha is a condensed formula using selected syllables. It belongs to the same wider world of recitation and sacred writing, while identifying a shorter form rather than an entire verse.

Related: Hua Chai Khatha · Akkhara · Bali

ปลุกเสก

Pluk Sek

Consecration

The consecration of an object through ritual, commonly involving recitation and concentrated intention.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗
Explore meaning & tradition

Consecrating a sacred object. Pluk sek describes ritual consecration, commonly involving recitation and concentrated intention. In the tradition, the rite establishes or strengthens an object’s sacred power. The object’s material and the rite performed over it are separate parts of its description.

Writing and blessing. An inscribed diagram can be discussed in terms of both long yant, the act of inscribing it, and pluk sek, its consecration. The vocabulary helps distinguish making the physical inscription from the associated ritual work.

Related ceremonies. Phutthaphisek names a Buddhist consecration ceremony involving monastic recitation and meditation. It is a more specific ceremony term. Knowing both words helps readers follow descriptions of how objects were made and blessed.

Related: Phutthaphisek

อาจารย์

Ajarn

Teacher

A teacher or instructor.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗

A title, not a single occupation. Ajarn is the ordinary Thai title for a teacher or instructor. In sacred tattooing and ritual settings, it identifies a teacher in the relevant practice. The title alone does not say that someone is a monk or specify their lineage or expertise.

The relationship around the title. Wai khru concerns honouring teachers, while kha khru names a teacher offering. These terms help explain the relationship between the recipient and the person transmitting a practice. Specific ceremonies and observances belong to the school or teacher concerned.

Related: Wai Khru · Kha Khru

ไหว้ครู

Wai Khru

Honouring teachers

Paying respect to teachers.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗
Explore meaning & tradition

Wai khru honours teachers and the transmission of knowledge. It pays respect to living teachers, earlier teachers in a lineage, and sacred teacher figures.

Teacher ceremonies occur in sacred tattooing, music, dance and other Thai arts. Offerings and the form of the ceremony follow the practice concerned.

Khan khru names an offering arrangement; kha khru is an offering acknowledging the teacher; khrop khru is a ceremony of initiation or transmission.

Related: Khan Khru · Kha Khru · Ajarn

Nuea

Material

The material or substance of an object.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗

Material in an amulet description. Although nuea has other meanings in everyday Thai, here it identifies the material or body of an object. Clay, powder-based mixtures, and metals are common distinctions in Buddhist-amulet descriptions.

Separate pieces of identification. Phim concerns the mould or design type; run concerns the issue or batch. Nuea concerns material. A useful description can give all three because they answer different questions about the same object.

Metta Maha Niyom

Goodwill and popularity

A common amulet and tattoo purpose: attracting kindness, goodwill, and favourable regard.

Hear Thai online ↗

Metta maha niyom attracts kindness, goodwill and favourable attention. It is sought in relationships, negotiation, work and trade.

Maha niyom emphasizes popularity; maha sane emphasizes attraction and allure. Goodwill need not mean romantic or sexual attraction.

These purposes occur across Buddhist amulets, charms, sacred tattoos, incantations and rituals. The purpose describes what the practice is used for, rather than naming one particular object or design.

Khlaeo Khlat

Avoidance of danger

Escaping or avoiding danger.

Hear Thai online ↗
Explore meaning & tradition

Khlaeo khlat is protection through escaping danger: an accident is avoided, an attack misses, or a threatening encounter passes safely.

It differs from khong kraphan, bodily invulnerability, and maha ut, weapon-stopping protection. All three fall within the wider vocabulary of protection, but describe different effects.

The same protective purpose can accompany a takrut, Buddhist amulet, tattoo or incantation.

Buddhist amulet vocabulary

Buddhist amulet types and forms

พระพิมพ์

Phra Phim

Votive tablets

A sacred image made in a mould, usually a small Buddhist tablet.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗

Votive tablets: small Buddha images pressed in molds and sealed inside stupas as merit. When old stupas collapsed or were opened, the tablets came out and were taken up as amulets. They are the ancestor of the Buddha amulet.

Related: Phra Khrueang · Phra Bucha · Kru

พระสมเด็จ

Somdet Buddha tablet

A well-known amulet type, commonly a rectangular powder tablet showing a seated Buddha beneath an arch.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗
Explore meaning & tradition

Somdet To of Wat Rakhang is closely connected with this amulet type. Sacred powder mixtures and a seated Buddha above a tiered base are familiar features. Phra Somdet is one of the five types in the collectors’ Benjaphakhi set.

Related: Benchaphakhi · Itthiche · Phathamang

พระปิดตา

Phra Pit Ta

Closed-eyes amulet

An amulet showing a seated figure covering its eyes.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗
Explore meaning & tradition

Phra Pit Ta is recognized by a seated figure covering its eyes. Examples appear in different materials and poses.

In the Maha Ut form, additional hands close the bodily openings, traditionally counted as nine. This form is connected with weapon-stopping protection.

Phra Pit Ta names an image type, while maha ut names a protective power. A form and its purpose can therefore appear together in an amulet description.

Related: Maha Ut

พระกริ่ง

Phra Kring

Rattling Buddha amulet

A small Buddha image containing a pellet that rattles when shaken.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗

A small cast Buddha modeled on the Medicine Buddha (Phra Phaisatchayakhuru), usually holding a medicine bowl, with a pellet inside that rattles. Because of the Medicine Buddha link it is kept for protection from illness, and some are placed in water to make holy water.

Related: Phutthaphisek · Nam Mon

A collectors’ grouping of five celebrated amulet types: Phra Somdet, Phra Rod, Phra Nang Phaya, Phra Phong Suphan, and Phra Sum Ko.

The five are Phra Somdet of Wat Rakhang, Phra Rod of Lamphun, Phra Nang Phaya of Phitsanulok, Phra Phong Suphan, and Phra Sum Ko of Kamphaeng Phet. The grouping belongs to amulet collecting; it is not a scale of Buddhist spiritual attainment.

เหรียญ

Rian

Medallions

A coin or medal.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗

A familiar word in a specific setting. Rian is the ordinary word for a coin or medal. In an amulet description it commonly identifies a metal medallion bearing a monk, the Buddha, or another revered figure. The collecting context supplies the sacred-object meaning.

Form, image, and issue. The portrait or design identifies what is represented; the material and production method identify other aspects of the piece. Run names an issue or batch. These details can distinguish medallions that share a similar subject.

A small pendant containing a picture or keepsake.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗

In amulet use, the picture often shows a revered monk or teacher. Portrait pendants are familiar objects in the amulet world.

รูปหล่อ

Rup Lo

Cast images

A cast image, often of a revered monk.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗
Explore meaning & tradition

A method of making. Rup lo means an image made by casting. It often describes a figure of a revered monk, but the term itself identifies the production form rather than one particular person or power.

Size and use. Small cast figures can be carried or worn as amulets; larger images may be kept for veneration. This is why rup lo and phra bucha can overlap in a description: one concerns making, the other the image’s use.

Altar images and sacred relics

พระบูชา

Phra Bucha

Altar Buddha images

A Buddha image intended for veneration on an altar or another dedicated place, rather than principally for wearing on the body.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗
Explore meaning & tradition

Altar image or worn amulet?. Phra bucha identifies a Buddha image intended for a place of veneration, such as an altar. Phra khrueang identifies the small Buddhist amulets commonly carried or worn. Both can depict the Buddha, so the intended setting is an important distinction.

Form and making. An altar image can also be described by its posture, material, and method of production. Rup lo means a cast image: it describes how a figure was made, whereas phra bucha describes its devotional role.

พระธาตุ

Phra That

Relics

Sacred relics, especially remains associated with the Buddha or revered Buddhist practitioners.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗
Explore meaning & tradition

Phra that refers to sacred relics of the Buddha and revered Buddhist practitioners. Relics are venerated in shrines, stupas and other devotional settings.

The word also occurs in the names of shrines or stupas housing relics. A sacred image represents a revered figure; a relic is venerated as remains associated with that figure.

พระเกศา

Phra Kesa

Revered hair relic

Hair of a revered religious figure.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗

Kesa means hair, with phra marking reverence. In sacred-object descriptions the phrase identifies hair kept as a relic or incorporated into an object.

Charm and sacred-object vocabulary

Scrolls, cloths and inscribed charms

ตะกรุด

Takrut

Rolled scroll charm

A rolled scroll charm, commonly made from a thin metal sheet inscribed with sacred letters or diagrams.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗
Explore meaning & tradition

A takrut is made from a surface inscribed with sacred characters or diagrams and rolled into a tube. Thin metal sheets are common; palm-leaf forms also occur.

Takrut can be carried or strung on cords worn around the neck, waist or arm. Single scrolls and sets of several scrolls have their own names.

An inscription or accompanying name identifies its purpose, such as protection or attraction. Metal, cord wrapping and lacquer describe other features of the same charm.

Related: Yant · Khatha · Lek Chan · Phitsamon

ผ้ายันต์

Pha Yant

Yantra cloth

Cloth bearing sacred diagrams or inscriptions.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗
Explore meaning & tradition

The sacred diagram on cloth. Pha yant names the surface as well as the inscription: a cloth bearing yantra diagrams, numerals, or sacred script. It can be displayed in a house or shop, folded and carried, or tied to the body. Its physical form makes it easier to recognize than a purpose word alone.

Regional purposes. Writing on Lanna yantra cloths records aims including auspiciousness, luck, goodwill, protection from spirits, protection from weapons, and protection from other dangers. These are classifications of attributed use. They do not imply that every cloth bears the same diagram or is used in the same way.

Connection to tattoos. Yant names the sacred diagram. Pha yant places it on cloth; sak yant places sacred diagrams or inscriptions on the body. The shared word explains the relationship between these practices while the complete names identify different physical forms.

Related: Yant · Yant Paet Thit

Child-spirit figures and skull-bone charms

กุมารทอง

Kuman Thong

Golden child spirit

A child-spirit figure kept in a relationship of care, offerings, and expected assistance.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗
Explore meaning & tradition

The older literary and ritual tradition describes a fetus taken from a woman who died while pregnant, dried by fire, lacquered and gilded. This is the background of the golden-child name.

Contemporary devotional figures are also made from powders, herbs and other materials. Keepers offer food, drinks and toys, treating the spirit as a child who helps guard the household or bring good fortune.

Kuman thong, prai kuman, rak yom and luk krok are related child-spirit terms with distinct meanings. Kuman thong does not automatically mean that a modern figure contains human remains.

Related: Prai Kuman · Luk Krok · Phi Tai Thang Klom

Rak yom names a pair of child-spirit figures traditionally carved from rak and mayom wood. The names of the two woods explain the pair’s name.

Figures are often kept with fragrant oil and offered food or other gifts. Their care forms part of the relationship between keeper and child spirits.

The paired form distinguishes rak yom from a single kuman thong figure.

Related: Kuman Thong · Prai Kuman

The act of inscribing. Long yant means writing or inscribing a sacred diagram. It names an action. Yant names the diagram, and the complete description identifies the surface, such as cloth or metal, and the writing involved.

อักษรขอม

Akson Khom

Khom script

A Khmer-derived writing tradition used in Thai religious manuscripts and sacred diagrams.

Hear Thai online ↗ See pictures ↗
Explore meaning & tradition

A writing system. Akson Khom refers to a Khmer-derived writing tradition used in Thai religious manuscripts and sacred diagrams. It names the script: the visual system of characters. It is useful vocabulary for recognizing writing on tattoos and inscribed objects.

Script is not language. A text written in Khom can be Pali. The script answers “what characters are these?” while the language answers “what language is written here?” Keeping the questions separate avoids assuming that every Khom inscription is a Khmer-language sentence.

Connected terms. Akkhara names letters or characters; khatha names a verse or incantation; yant names a sacred diagram. A single inscription may involve all three. Different regional practices also use other scripts, including Lanna Tham.

“Golden tongue.” In charm terminology, a motif associated with attractive or persuasive speech.

Fortune, trade, and prosperity

โชคลาภ

Chok Lap

Luck and fortunate gain

Good fortune, especially the prospect of receiving something beneficial or valuable.

Hear Thai online ↗
Context & tradition

In amulet, yantra, and ritual descriptions, this names a hoped-for benefit. It can concern an unexpected gain as well as prosperity more broadly; the word itself does not identify a particular object or ceremony.

ค้าขาย

Kha Khai

Trade and business

Trading or doing business; in charm descriptions, the wish for successful sales and commerce.

Hear Thai online ↗
Context & tradition

This is an ordinary Thai expression used in descriptions of charms and rituals for shopkeepers and other traders. Goodwill, persuasive speech, attracting customers, and prosperity may be described together in this context.

Related purposes: Fortune and business
